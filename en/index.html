01About the research

What this is.
A quantitative report on 78 years of Israeli governance, 1948—2026. Each unit of analysis — prime minister, public system, social sector, company, NGO — receives a single score (F) summarizing 17 sub-indicators, all drawn from open and verifiable primary sources.
Why it is needed.
Public debate about government performance tends to be dichotomous (good/bad), event-driven (one defining event), or partisan. This research examines systemic function — consistent across periods, across units of analysis, and independent of political identity.
What is new.
Uniform application of the Data Fluidity methodology across five parallel units of analysis; direct comparison of all 13 prime ministers on identical metrics; contribution-vs-extraction (NCR) analysis of the 100 largest companies and 30,000 NGOs; full reproducibility (code + data).
What this is not.
Not a legal judgment. Not an assessment of individual intent. Not a substitute for public elections. A synthetic index intended to support evidence-based debate, not to close it.
Who wrote it.
Independent research by enh@hemligroups.com under the MIT license. The researcher has no affiliation with any party, government, or public institution. The 40 source documents, 17 calculation algorithms, and raw data are publicly available at github.com/udihem78-maker/animated-eureka.

02Methodology — how we measure governance

The core idea

Governance, at any level, is the process of translating decisions into public benefit. The smoother and more efficient the translation, the better the function. Ehud Hemli's Data Fluidity methodology quantifies this flow in three components:

Component What it measures High score = Low score =
DDecision quality Quality of decisions made Evidence-based, coordinated, deliberate Impulsive, political, uncoordinated
UUtility realized Public benefit actually delivered Decisions reach the public and have effect Decisions remain on paper
RResistance Systemic friction along the path Smooth, efficient bureaucracy Entangled, wasteful, blocking bureaucracy

The formula

The composite score for each unit of analysis: F = (D × U) / R. The better the decisions (D) and the greater the realized utility (U) relative to friction (R), the higher the F score. Scale: 0 to 100. A score of 100 = ideal system (not observed in practice). Above 25 = functional governance. Below 12 = collapse threshold.

The 17 sub-indicators

The D, U, R components themselves are composed from 14 sub-indicators — 17 quantitative metrics in total. Each metric is computed from 3 to 15 raw data points drawn from primary sources.

03Measurement process & data sources

Working process · five steps

  1. Collection. Raw data pulled from 16 primary sources (listed below). Original pulls, no intermediate aggregators.
  2. Cross-verification. Every figure confirmed against at least two independent sources. Discrepancies are documented, not smoothed over.
  3. Normalization. Currencies converted to 2026 ILS, CPI-adjusted, demographically and period-adjusted.
  4. Computation. The 17 measurement algorithms are applied to the normalized data. Open source under MIT license.
  5. Aggregation. F score composed from D, U, R components for each of the 1,350 units of analysis.

16 primary sources

  1. Israel Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS) — statistical yearbooks, 1948—2026
  2. Bank of Israel — annual reports and banking-system breakdowns
  3. Office of the State Comptroller — 85+ annual reports, 1995—2026
  4. Israel Securities Authority — public-company filings and prospectuses
  5. Registrar of Companies — ownership structures and control changes
  6. Knesset website (knesset.gov.il) — protocols, votes, parliamentary queries
  7. State Attorney's Office yearbooks — conviction rates and investigation duration
  8. OECD Economic Surveys of Israel (2001—2024)
  9. IMF Article IV Consultation Reports — Israel
  10. World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators (WGI)
  11. V-Dem Institute, University of Gothenburg — Varieties of Democracy
  12. Freedom House — press freedom and democracy indices
  13. Transparency International — Corruption Perceptions Index
  14. Israel Democracy Institute — Israeli Democracy Index
  15. Taub Center for Social Policy Studies — annual compendia
  16. Think-tank publications (incl. Kohelet Forum, for polarity analysis)

What this research does not use. No opinion polling as a primary source, no social media, no press reporting (except for cross-verification). Only sources from which the computation can be reproduced.

04Scope & limitations

Research scope

  • 13prime ministers · 16 distinct entries (Rabin ×2, Netanyahu ×6)
  • 120active MKs · 1,250 leadership figures tracked
  • 15public systems (military, police, education, health, welfare, courts, rabbinate…)

  • 30,000NGOs (contribution-vs-extraction analysis)

Limitations

(a) The index is a synthetic aggregate — a planning instrument, not a legal determination. (b) F scores for periods before 1990 are reconstructed from secondary sources with wider confidence intervals. (c) Cumulative fiscal-damage figures are estimates based on debt, lost output, and human-capital calculations — not accounting determinations. (d) The research measures aggregate systemic outcomes, not individual intent. (e) The report is independent and does not represent any institution.

Reproducibility

The 40 source documents, 17 calculation algorithms, and raw underlying data are publicly available under the MIT license: github.com/udihem78-maker/animated-eureka. Every number in this report traces back to its source.

A settler in ultra-hard Religious Zionism lives in a system with F=26.7 — above the average German (F=18). These are two different systems inside the same state. Israel 2026 is Denmark (for settlers) and South Sudan (for Bedouin) at the same time.

Relation to national F (0.6). The low national F is the weighted mean of enormous variance — not a score of "one failing state." Israel 2026's F is the geometric mean of 23 different states in one, some functioning (F=27), some collapsing (F=1), and the system meant to serve them all averages out to 0.6.

25.7 Local-authority fractal laws

  • Law of gaps: F of an authority ≈ (subsidy/resident × 2 + leadership score / 10) / 2. The higher the subsidy, the higher the F — even when management is weak.

  • Law of discrimination: Arab cities and Bedouin villages — F = average F × 0.3. A gap that hasn't closed in 78 years.

29.3 Anomaly · "working poor"

40% of Israel's poor are working. This isn't a population "not seeking work" — it's work that doesn't pay enough to live on. Minimum wage (₪5,400/mo) is below the poverty line for a family of 4 (₪14,800). A couple both on minimum wage earns ₪10,800/mo — ₪4,000 below the poverty line. The NII can't close this: it has no "wage benefit." This is a wage-policy problem, not a benefit-policy problem.
