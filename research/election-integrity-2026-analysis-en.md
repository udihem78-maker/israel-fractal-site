## 0 · Method

The approach: not to establish facts, but to **rank claims by their alignment with observable data**. Each claim is scored on four dimensions:

- **E (Evidence quality)** — quality of the evidence presented (0—10)
- **A (Alignment with data)** — fit with observable field measurements (0—10)
- **I (Independent verification)** — confirmation by a neutral source (0—10)
- **M (Mechanism plausibility)** — is the alleged mechanism realistic (0—10)
- **Score** = E · A · I · M / 1000 (0—10)

Score ≥ 7 = substantiated · 4—6.9 = plausible but unproven · 2—3.9 = weak · < 2 = unfounded.

**This analysis reaches no political conclusion**. It shows which claims pass the data test and which do not.

## 3 · Claim-by-claim analysis

### T1 — Qatar transferred tens of millions ₪ to Arab lists
- **E · evidence quality:** 3/10 — based on anonymous Shin Bet leaks, not official statement
- **A · data alignment:** 5/10 — a +22-point turnout jump *could* indicate a funded mobilization, but equally a sectoral backlash against the current government
- **I · independent verification:** 2/10 — no official confirmation. Shin Bet "does not comment"
- **M · mechanism plausibility:** 7/10 — Qatar has funded civil-society entities in Israel before (Al Jazeera, NGOs). History allows it. A transfer to boost turnout is technically possible
- **Score: 3 · 5 · 2 · 7 / 1000 = 0.21 → score 2.1 (weak)**
- **Conclusion:** possible claim but unproven. Requires concrete evidence (bank records · transfer documentation · witnesses).

### T2 — Turkish money (Muslim Brotherhood)
- E 2 · A 4 · I 1 · M 6 → **score 0.48 (very weak)**
- **Conclusion:** the claim appeared after the Qatar claim. A familiar pattern of allegation expansion without additional evidence.

### T3 — 6 Ra'am activists with ₪10K
- E 7 · A 6 · I 8 · M 5 → **score 1.68 (weak)**
- ₪10,000 for 6 activists = ₪1,667 each. A sum too small for systemic "village vote-buying"
- The activists' alternative explanation ("legitimate campaign donation") has not been excluded
- **Conclusion:** valid arrest, but context not established. The sum is negligible against a multi-million campaign.

### T4 — Joint List MK received Qatari money
- E 2 · A 3 · I 1 · M 6 → **score 0.04 (unsupported)**
- **Conclusion:** anonymous leak without an identified MK. Pattern of generic allegation.

### T5 — Voting slips disappeared (Ra'am complaint)
- E 5 · A 4 · I 3 · M 7 → **score 0.42 (weak)**
- Documented complaint but scope not established. Pattern opposite to T1—T4.

### T6 — 20K invalid ballots
- E 10 · A 2 · I 10 · M 2 → **score 0.40 (normal baseline)**
- 20K out of ~5M = 0.4% — **below** the historical average (0.8—1.1%)
- **Conclusion:** no anomaly. The implied claim ("disqualification anomaly") is not supported by the data

### T7 — "Elections stolen"
- E 0 · A 0 · I 0 · M 2 → **score 0 (political statement · not an empirical claim)**
- Netanyahu received 53 mandates — a gain relative to pre-election polls (50). Gain + Presidential confidence ≠ theft
- **Conclusion:** political rhetoric, not a claim that can be evaluated

## 4 · Findings that **align** vs findings that **contradict**

- ✓ Sharp +22—33 point turnout increases in specific localities
- ✓ 83—74% concentration on a single list (Rahat + Kafr Qasim) — historically anomalous

## 7 · The falsification test

**The claim is falsified** if it turns out that:
- a documented transfer > ₪5M from Qatar to an Arab party did not occur, and
- the turnout shift (beyond 10 points) was not impossible without the funding, and
- the connection was documented in real time, not retroactively

**The claim is confirmed** if it turns out that:
- all 3 above held with concrete evidence (bank record + transfer + testimony)

Otherwise — the claim remains "unsubstantiated" and not "refuted". Based on the data known as of Nov 3, 2026, there is insufficient data to decide.
